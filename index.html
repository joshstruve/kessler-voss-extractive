The moon was just sitting there.

Eleven years ago, Kessler-Voss Extractive looked at 3.8 billion years of undisturbed lunar regolith and asked the only reasonable question: what's it worth. Today we run three active sites, process fourteen thousand tons of material a day, and supply the elements your grandchildren's reactors will run on.

MISSION

We don't explore the moon. We process it.

Exploration was the twentieth century's approach to the moon. Ours is extraction at scale. Kessler-Voss operates the largest off-world industrial footprint in cislunar space — three surface sites, one polar harvesting operation, and a processing capacity that outstrips every other lunar concern combined. We are not interested in flags. We are interested in yield.

Our extraction stack begins with autonomous mass-driver rovers that strip and sort regolith by density and mineral content before it ever reaches a human hand. Thermal volatile extraction units bake water ice and trapped gases out of polar shadow material at controlled sub-zero gradients, while our sintering refineries convert raw regolith into structural-grade metal stock on-site.

None of this is without risk. We've said so publicly, and we'll say it again here: lunar extraction at this scale has no precedent. We proceed anyway, because someone was always going to, and we intend for it to be us.
